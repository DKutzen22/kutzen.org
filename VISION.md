# Vision for kutzen.org

## Purpose

kutzen.org is my personal home on the web: a place to communicate
the things that my LinkedIn profile and résumé cannot.

It should introduce me clearly, then invite people to discover
more through my projects, research, blog posts, and poetry.

The goal is not just to show what I have done. It is to show
what I am passionate about, how I think, and what I choose
to create and explore.

## Central idea

The website should feel like half polished portfolio and half
poetry notebook, with room for the research and projects I love.

This describes its character, not a requirement to divide the
site into equal halves.

It should feel considered and presentable without becoming
impersonal. It should leave room for creativity and reflection
without becoming confusing.

My professional, technical, and creative interests belong to
the same person. The website should let them coexist without
forcing everything into a professional brand.

## Audience

The site is for:
- Future employers and coworkers who want to understand my
  work, interests, and approach.
- Anyone curious about me who wants to explore what I make,
  study, think about, or write.

Visitors should not need to share my technical background
to understand who I am or why I care about my work.

People looking for professional information should find it
directly. People exploring out of curiosity should feel
equally welcome.

## Visitor experience

The site should introduce me before asking visitors to
navigate a collection of content.

From that introduction, its organization should draw people
deeper into who I am.

A visitor should be able to:
1. Get an initial sense of who I am.
2. Recognize the interests and kinds of work represented here.
3. Follow something that catches their attention.
4. Explore it in greater depth.
5. Discover other parts of my work or writing.

Not everyone needs to follow the same path or read everything.
The site should offer clear entry points and meaningful
connections without requiring a prescribed journey.

## What the site should communicate

Visitors should be able to discover:
- What I am passionate about.
- What I choose to build, study, and explore.
- How I approach problems and creative work.
- The ideas and experiences I express through writing.
- The person behind the projects and credentials.

These qualities should emerge through substantive work,
thoughtful explanations, and authentic writing rather than
generic self-promotional claims.

## Content

### Projects

Projects should have room for depth beyond a résumé bullet
or repository description.

A project page should be able to explain:
- What the project is.
- Why I cared about it.
- What I contributed or built.
- How I approached it.
- What I learned.
- Its current state and limitations.

Technical details, images, results, and links should be
included where they help tell that story.

### Research

The site should provide space for the research I care about,
including the questions, motivations, and context behind it.

Research content should support deeper exploration while
giving unfamiliar readers an accessible point of entry.

### Blog posts

Blog posts can include explanations, observations,
reflections, and other ideas I want to share.

They do not all need to serve a professional purpose.

### Poetry

Poetry is an intentional part of the site.

It should have room to exist as creative writing, without
needing to demonstrate a professional skill or connect
to a technical project.

### Résumé and CV

My résumé and CV should be easy to find.

They provide a concise, formal account of my background.
The rest of the site provides the depth, personality,
and context that those documents cannot.

### Social links

Visitors should be able to find my relevant social profiles
and other places where I share work or participate online.

### Future additions

The site should remain a platform for other things I want
to portray, publish, or show.

Its organization should accommodate new interests without
requiring a speculative feature or section for every possibility.

## Guiding principles

### Clarity over flair

Visitors should be able to understand the site and find
their way around it.

Visual effects, unusual layouts, and interactions should
not obscure content or make basic tasks harder.

When expression and usability conflict, preserve clarity.

### Creativity over trend following

Design choices should reflect my interests, sensibilities,
and content rather than imitate whatever is currently popular.

A familiar pattern is welcome when it works. An unconventional
choice is welcome when it has a purpose.

Neither novelty nor conformity is a goal by itself.

### Introduce, then invite depth

Give visitors enough context to orient themselves before
presenting detailed material.

Make deeper content available without overwhelming the
first impression or flattening everything into summaries.

### Show passion through substance

Let the selection of work, the depth of explanation, and
the writing demonstrate what matters to me.

Do not substitute exaggerated claims for concrete examples.

### Preserve my voice

The site should sound like me, not like a corporate portfolio
template or a generic personal brand.

Professional presentation should not erase personality.

### Let different content take different forms

A project explanation, research page, blog post, and poem
do not need identical presentation.

They should feel part of the same site while respecting
the needs of the content.

## Non-goals

- Recreating LinkedIn as a personal website.
- Turning my résumé into a longer webpage.
- Making the whole site feel like a job application.
- Making every piece of writing justify its professional value.
- Hiding creative interests to appear more professional.
- Following design trends at the expense of personal expression.
- Using visual complexity as a substitute for meaningful content.

## Success criteria

The site succeeds when:
- It feels like a genuine representation of me.
- A new visitor can understand who I am and where to explore next.
- Future employers and coworkers can find relevant work and
  professional information without unnecessary searching.
- Curious visitors can discover interests and writing beyond
  my professional background.
- Projects and research support meaningful depth.
- Blog posts and poetry feel like intentional parts of the site.
- My résumé, CV, and social links are easy to find.
- The site gives me room to share new work and interests.
