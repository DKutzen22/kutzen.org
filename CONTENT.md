# Content & Voice for kutzen.org

## Purpose

The content on kutzen.org should communicate what my LinkedIn
profile and résumé cannot: what I care about, how I think,
and the depth behind my work and writing.

The site serves future employers and coworkers, as well as
anyone curious about me.

It should introduce me clearly and offer paths into deeper
material without making every page feel like a job application.

## Relationship to Other Documents

- VISION.md defines the site's purpose and priorities.
- DESIGN.md defines its visual presentation.
- This document defines voice, content expectations, and
  editorial boundaries.
- SITEMAP.md defines where content belongs and how it connects.
- AGENTS.md defines how an agent should work in the repository.

## Voice

### Personal, clear, and substantive

Write as a person communicating with another person.

Prefer direct language and specific explanations over formal
self-promotion or generic portfolio copy.

Let enthusiasm emerge through what I choose to discuss and
the attention I give it.

### Professional without becoming corporate

Professional content should be thoughtful, accurate, and
well organized without sounding like a marketing statement.

Do not turn ordinary experience into inflated claims.

Avoid stock phrases such as:
- "Passionate innovator."
- "Leveraging cutting-edge solutions."
- "Results-driven professional."
- "At the forefront of..."
- "Revolutionizing..."

If a claim matters, support it with an example or explanation.

### Curious without pretending certainty

Make room for exploration, unfinished ideas, and things
I am still learning.

Distinguish what I know, what I infer, what I am testing,
and what remains unresolved.

Do not turn a tentative observation into a definitive claim.

### Different registers, one person

The site does not need a single rigid tone.

A project explanation can be precise and technical.
A blog post can be exploratory or conversational.
A personal essay can be reflective.
A poem can follow its own voice and structure.

Maintain authenticity rather than forcing every piece
to sound like portfolio copy.

## Point of View

Use first person for my experiences, motivations,
decisions, and reflections unless the context calls
for a different perspective.

Do not use "we" for work I completed independently.

For collaborative work, distinguish my contribution
from the contributions of the group.

## Introduce, Then Invite Depth

Give readers enough context to understand what they
are looking at before presenting specialized detail.

For technical content:
- Explain the problem before the implementation.
- Introduce specialized terms when needed.
- Make the significance understandable without requiring
  every reader to follow every technical detail.
- Preserve depth for readers who want it.

Do not oversimplify the work merely to make it sound accessible.

## Factual Integrity

Do not invent or embellish:
- Credentials or qualifications.
- Affiliations or positions.
- Publications or awards.
- Project capabilities.
- Performance measurements.
- Research findings.
- Collaborators or contributions.
- Testimonials or endorsements.
- Dates, milestones, or completion status.

Use information I have supplied or confirmed source material.

When a fact is missing:
- Ask for it when it is necessary.
- Otherwise, use a clearly marked draft placeholder.
- Do not publish unresolved placeholders.

Distinguish between:
- An idea.
- An experiment.
- Work in progress.
- A functioning implementation.
- Completed or published work.

Do not describe a planned feature as an existing capability.

## Content Types

### Personal Introduction

The introduction should establish who I am and give visitors
a reason to explore further.

It should connect my interests without becoming a list of
every technology, hobby, or qualification.

Keep the initial introduction focused. Let deeper pages
carry the fuller story.

### About Page

The About page should provide context that a short introduction
cannot: my interests, motivations, background, and approach.

It should help readers understand how biology, software,
systems, and creative work fit into my life.

Do not manufacture a seamless career narrative or pretend
every interest exists to support one professional goal.

### Project Pages

Project pages should go beyond résumé bullets and feature lists.

Where relevant, explain:
- What the project is.
- What problem or question motivated it.
- Why I cared about it.
- My role and contributions.
- How I approached the work.
- Important decisions and tradeoffs.
- What currently works.
- Limitations or unfinished areas.
- What I learned.
- Where readers can find code, documentation, or related work.

Use this as a flexible guide, not a mandatory heading
template for every project.

Prioritize meaningful explanation over making every project
appear equally large or impressive.

### Research Content

Explain the question, context, and my contribution.

Distinguish established background from my own results,
interpretations, and open questions.

Describe scientific and computational methods accurately.
Do not imply that software output alone establishes
a biological conclusion.

Clearly identify preliminary or unpublished work.

Do not publish material whose sharing permissions
or confidentiality are unresolved.

### Blog: Reading Frames

Reading Frames is a place for technical articles, essays,
notes, explanations, and reflections.

Posts do not need to serve a hiring or networking purpose.

A post may document a finished solution, investigate an idea,
or record an ongoing learning process. Make its status clear.

For technical posts, include enough context to distinguish
a reusable explanation from a record of one particular setup.

Do not add personal anecdotes, opinions, or experiences
that I have not supplied.

### Poetry

Poetry is an intentional part of the site, not supplementary
professional content.

Preserve:
- Wording.
- Punctuation.
- Capitalization.
- Line breaks.
- Stanza boundaries.
- Intentional spacing.

Do not add explanations, interpretations, summaries,
or decorative quotations unless requested.

Do not rewrite a poem to improve search visibility,
make it sound more polished, or fit a layout.

If a formatting limitation affects a poem, flag it rather
than silently changing the work.

### Résumé and CV

The site should clearly distinguish my résumé from
my academic CV.

Descriptions and download labels should be straightforward.

Do not add qualifications to surrounding website copy
that are unsupported by my approved background.

Flag inconsistencies between documents rather than
guessing which version is correct.

### Contact and Social Links

Use direct, welcoming language.

Do not invent availability, response-time promises,
collaboration policies, or preferred contact methods.

Use only confirmed destinations and account identifiers.

## Titles, Summaries & Labels

Titles should describe the content or express its character
without relying on clickbait.

Summaries should help visitors decide whether to read further.
Do not repeat the title without adding useful context.

Navigation labels, buttons, and download links should make
their destination or action clear.

Creative titles are welcome where they do not make the
site difficult to navigate.

## Editorial Boundaries

These are proposed defaults until explicitly revised.

### Agent-Drafted Site Copy

An agent may propose introductions, project descriptions,
page summaries, and interface copy based on confirmed facts.

Treat substantial new copy as a draft for review.

### Existing Technical Writing

Preserve the meaning, degree of certainty, and technical detail.

Flag proposed changes that alter a conclusion, interpretation,
or account of my contribution.

Do not replace specific explanations with generic prose
merely to shorten or smooth the writing.

### Personal Essays and Poetry

Treat these as authored work rather than general site copy.

Without an explicit editing request:
- Do not substantially rewrite them.
- Do not normalize distinctive phrasing.
- Do not change their perspective or emotional meaning.
- Suggest suspected corrections rather than silently applying them.

### Personal Voice

Never fabricate experiences, feelings, beliefs, or motivations
and present them as mine.

Ask when the content requires a personal perspective
that I have not provided.

## Content Review Checklist

Before publishing or substantially revising content, check:

- Is it accurate?
- Does it sound like a person rather than generic marketing copy?
- Is my contribution represented honestly?
- Is the work's current status clear?
- Does the introduction provide enough context?
- Is deeper detail available where it matters?
- Are technical claims and uncertainties represented carefully?
- Have intentional creative choices been preserved?
- Are links and download destinations correct?
- Are placeholders or unsupported claims still present?
